# How FlipFirst estimates value — DRAFT, NOT PUBLISHED

*Long form of the disclaimer the deal page already shows ("Estimated, not
guaranteed or independently verified…").*

- **Where the number comes from.** FlipFirst compares a car with similar
  listings: same make and model, model year within one or two years, the
  same trim and duty class when stated, similar mileage, nearby when
  possible. Most comparisons use other sellers' **asking prices**, which are
  not what cars sold for; the page says which evidence was used, and the
  range is widened to reflect that.
- **Confidence.** High, medium or low reflects how much evidence there is
  and how well it matches — not a probability, and not yet calibrated
  against actual sales. Without completed-sale evidence, confidence is at
  most medium.
- **What it can't see.** Condition, accident history, title problems,
  modifications and local demand that the listing doesn't state. Listings
  that mention damage, a branded title, an auction bid, implausible mileage
  or scam signals are labeled and kept out of the trusted feed — but a
  listing can omit problems.
- **Profit estimates** subtract assumed costs (negotiation, repairs, fees,
  transport, holding time) from an estimated resale value. You can change
  the assumptions. Actual results depend on the car and on you.
- **Not advice.** FlipFirst doesn't inspect cars or verify titles,
  ownership or history, and isn't an appraiser or financial adviser. Inspect
  the car, check the title and history, and decide for yourself.
