# Data provider decision

Researched 2026-10-28, revised 2026-10-30 by web search. **Direct page
fetches to vendor sites (developer.ebay.com, docs.marketcheck.com) are
blocked by this build environment's network policy**, so everything below
comes from search results quoting vendor pages. Nothing here was confirmed
with a live API call or with a vendor. Engineering research, not legal
advice.

## What changed on 2026-10-30

1. eBay is not click-through: production Buy API use needs an eBay Partner
   Network application, eBay's approval and contracts (no guarantee), and a
   keyset activated through account-deletion notifications or an exemption.
2. **eBay's API License Agreement prohibits using eBay content "to suggest
   or model prices for items listed on eBay Sites"** and requires written
   permission for derived average prices (search excerpts of the official
   page). FlipFirst's value estimate for an eBay listing is such a price
   model, so eBay needs **written permission for price estimates** on top of
   production access. Details: `EBAY_SETUP.md`.
3. A real deal needs two licensed things, not one: **listings** and a
   **market value** to compare them with. In commercial mode FlipFirst only
   values a car from licensed comparables; without them every car shows
   "not enough data".

## Decision

1. **Ask MarketCheck first** (free to ask): one written agreement could
   cover both listings and values. Send the inquiry (`OWNER_ACTIONS.md`
   § 1) and buy nothing until it answers in writing.
2. **Apply to eBay in parallel** (free), asking explicitly for price-estimate
   permission. Treat it as a long shot until eBay answers.
3. **Recruit sellers** for real local cars FlipFirst owns the rights to;
   value them through the vendor from step 1 (or VinAudit by quote).
4. Nothing unlicensed: Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace and similar stay off.

How to read it:

- **Seller listings rank first on rights, freshness and cost — and have no
  supply on day one** (private-party coverage 1). They are the moat, not
  the launch source.
- **eBay and MarketCheck FSBO are now level** (59.6 vs 59.2), both gated on
  an agreement. eBay is free and integrated but thin on private-party cars;
  MarketCheck covers private sellers but costs at least $299/mo and its
  quota and radius limits are unclear.
- Craigslist scores high on raw supply and is excluded by the rights gate.

## What remains unverified (ask MarketCheck)

1. Whether storing FSBO listings and price history, polling centrally, and
   sending alerts to paying subscribers is permitted — and for how long data
   may be retained.
2. Which marketplaces feed FSBO (classifieds vs. dealer-adjacent), and how
   many active listings exist within 100 mi of our launch ZIPs.
3. Actual refresh latency (near-real-time vs 24 h) and whether price changes
   produce updated records.
4. Whether `first_seen_at`/`last_seen_at`-style fields exist on FSBO results
   (needed to sort by newest and detect drops cheaply).
5. Attribution/display rules for the listing URL and photos (cached images
   documented at ≤ 4 h private caching).
6. Startup / pilot pricing below $299/mo, or a free-tier evaluation that
   includes the FSBO endpoint (free tier reportedly excludes third-party APIs).
7. The real per-call price ($0.01 or $0.006), the monthly call allowance
   and whether the plan limits the search radius (100 mi reported).

Draft inquiry: `OWNER_ACTIONS.md` § 1.

## Polling budget (central, not per subscriber)

One territory = one `zip` + `radius` query sorted newest first, 50 rows/call.

| Poll interval | Calls / territory / month | Data fee @ $0.01 (unverified) | Fits 5,000 calls? (unverified) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 min | 720 | $7.20 | yes (6 territories) |
| 30 min | 1,440 | $14.40 | yes (3 territories) |
| 15 min | 2,880 | $28.80 | 1 territory |

Subscribers never trigger provider calls: matching runs against the shared
ingested data. Cost scales with **territories × poll frequency**, not users.
(Pagination beyond the first page only when the newest page is all new.)
